# UEQ-Lite — Lightweight User Experience Questionnaire

## Overview
Based on the User Experience Questionnaire, adapted for development-time evaluation. 
Focuses on the subjective quality of the user experience across both pragmatic (usability) 
and hedonic (enjoyment) dimensions. Best for content-heavy products, creative tools, 
and products where emotional engagement matters as much as utility.

## Dimensions

### 1. Attractiveness (Weight: 0.20)
Overall impression — does the product look and feel appealing?
- Rules should focus on: visual hierarchy, color harmony, whitespace, typography quality,
  image/icon quality, first-impression design

### 2. Perspicuity (Weight: 0.20)
Is the product easy to understand and learn?
- Rules should focus on: clear labels, intuitive navigation, visible system status,
  self-explanatory icons, minimal jargon, helpful tooltips

### 3. Efficiency (Weight: 0.20)
Can users achieve their goals without unnecessary effort?
- Rules should focus on: task flow optimization, minimal clicks, smart defaults,
  bulk actions, search quality, filtering capabilities

### 4. Dependability (Weight: 0.15)
Does the user feel in control and secure?
- Rules should focus on: predictable behavior, undo/redo, auto-save, 
  clear feedback on actions, data safety communication

### 5. Stimulation (Weight: 0.15)
Is the product exciting and motivating to use?
- Rules should focus on: micro-animations, progress indicators, achievement moments,
  personalization, surprising delightful details

### 6. Novelty (Weight: 0.10)
Does the product feel innovative and creative?
- Rules should focus on: unique interaction patterns, creative solutions to common problems,
  differentiated visual design, fresh approaches to standard features

## When to Use
- Content platforms (blogs, media, news)
- Creative tools (design, writing, music)
- Portfolio / showcase sites
- Products competing on experience quality
- Products where "feel" matters as much as function

## Scoring
Each dimension scored -3 to +3 (negative = bad, 0 = neutral, positive = good).
Normalized to 0–100 for consistency with other frameworks.
- 80–100: Excellent — users love the experience
- 60–79: Good — positive impression, some room for polish
- 40–59: Neutral — functional but unremarkable
- Below 40: Negative — users have a poor impression
